Above the fold
The phone’s first screen holds the whole promise: what it is, the headline, one button, and the four assistants. No menu: the page is one scroll, and its links live in the footer.
The graph, re-laid for a phone
The same twelve notes and links as desktop, rearranged tall instead of wide. Only the four notes the recall touches carry labels, at 13 px; the rest stay a quiet field. Same 6.5 s story.
One column, one action
Every section ends in the same "Start with your email". pip drops out of the hero on phones (no one installs from a phone); "Run it yourself" is a quiet link instead.
Fewer words, same story
Each paragraph is shorter than desktop’s. The proof strip becomes a 2 × 2 grid; the recall panel is cropped to where the action is, so its labels stay readable.
Tap and type
16 px gutters, 44 px minimum targets, 52 px buttons, body text at 15–16 px, no horizontal scroll at 390.
Shneiderman, applied
Consistency: one button, one accent, one mark. Feedback and closure: the panel finishes its answer before it loops. Reduce memory load: the technical sections sit behind one "For developers" link. The viewer itself follows his mantra: overview first, zoom and filter, then details on demand.
