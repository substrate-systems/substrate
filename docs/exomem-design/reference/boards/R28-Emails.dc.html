The slots, top to bottom
01Sender and subject From “Exomem”, or “Alex via Exomem” when a person sent it. Always exomem@substratesystems.io; replies go to support. The subject says what happened, in 45 characters or fewer.
02Preheader The one fact that matters next: a time limit, a price, a consequence.
03State band The web, in the account’s state. One of six, below. In production a 1200 × 240 PNG, decorative (empty alt).
04Wordmark The E-initial wordmark, 16 px. Never the icon alone.
05Headline IBM Plex Mono 24. Six words or fewer. It names the state, not the action.
06Body Inter 15 / 1.6. One or two short paragraphs in plain, simple English. No jargon, no exclamation marks.
07A note or facts Optional, never both. A note is a person’s own words with their name. Facts are label and value rows: price, until, size.
08One action One button. At most one text link beside it. Never a second button.
09Small print Every time limit, computed when it is sent, in the reader’s time zone. Say what happens if they ignore it.
10Footer “Exomem by Substrate Systems”. These are service emails, so no unsubscribe; any marketing email adds one.
Delivery
Each email also has a plain-text part with the same words. The HTML is 600 px wide with solid dark backgrounds and color-scheme set to dark, so a client that forces light mode still reads it. Web fonts often don’t load in mail, so IBM Plex Mono falls back to Menlo or Consolas and Inter to Helvetica or Arial. The band is a PNG, not SVG, because Gmail strips SVG.

Friends: the invitation family
An invitation is tied to one email address and works once. Opening it signs the person in, so there is no second sign-in email. A friend price is a Paddle discount attached to the invitation; Paddle shows it at checkout, and the start page and Home say where it came from. A covered invitation has no checkout at all.

Light inboxes
Dark is the default. The same email carries a light version in a prefers-color-scheme block, so Apple Mail, iOS Mail and Outlook for Mac show it to people who use light mode. Amber turns deep (#A86E00) so lit points still stand out on paper. Gmail ignores the block and may invert colours itself; the solid colours keep it readable either way.
