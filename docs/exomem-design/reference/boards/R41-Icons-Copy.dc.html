Which files, and the rules that come with them
Claude, Claude CodeAnthropic’s marks may be used only as Anthropic permits, in materials it approves first, unaltered (no colour, font or proportion changes), and Anthropic supplies the files. So: write to marketing@anthropic.com with this board, ask for the Claude mark and whichever mark they want for Claude Code, and ask for a monochrome version for dark and light.
ChatGPT, CodexOpenAI’s brand page: use the logo exactly as provided, no added colours, never more prominent than your own brand; co-branded material needs approval (partnercomms@openai.com). Files come from OpenAI’s brand page. The OpenAI logo comes in black and white versions, which fit the one-accent rule as they are. Ask which mark they want for Codex.
ColourMonochrome only, in the vendor’s own black or white file: white on dark, black on light. Never recoloured by us, never amber. If a vendor will only allow its colour mark, that’s a second colour on the page, and it comes back to you as a decision; don’t ship it by default.
Size and weightMark at most the cap height of the name next to it, and always smaller than the Exomem mark on the same screen (OpenAI asks for this; it’s right for us too).
Until approvedShip names only, exactly as today. The slot is reserved in the component (the chip’s padding and the tile’s centre), so turning marks on later is a file drop, not a layout change.
How they shipOne SVG per mark per theme, as provided, kept in the repo with a note of where it came from and the approval. No inline redraws, no icon fonts, no CDN hotlinks.
The sent step
Today it promises a link, but someone on the waitlist gets a waitlist email, not a link. The proposal says only what’s true for every address: an email is on its way and it says what happens next. Same words, same button, same timing for everyone; no queue position anywhere.

Why the flow graph looks soft on the OLED
Mostly ours. The flow pages lay the graph out as divs at 1280 px (390 on phone) and scale the whole layer to the screen with a transform; links are 1 px divs rotated, and the lit note grows with scale(1.7). While anything in that layer animates, the browser keeps it as a bitmap and scales the bitmap (1.125× at 1440 px wide, some odd factor on every phone), and a rotated 1 px div is an anti-aliased box at the best of times: lines land between pixels and go soft. The Home band and the landing hero are real SVG, which is why they look sharper. A little is the panel: a 1 px line at 7% on near-black is close to where OLEDs crush dark greys. Fix (Alex agreed): draw the same field as one SVG: same positions, same 7.5% links, same line weight, notes grown by radius instead of scale. Nothing about the look changes except sharpness.
